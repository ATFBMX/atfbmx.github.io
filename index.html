Who We Are

Part of a Canadian tradition

Canada has always produced flatland riders at the very top of the sport. Chase Gouin, out of Ontario, is one of the most influential flatland riders in the history of BMX, and his name is written into the sport’s history books (23mag archive).

The ATF picks up that thread. We are the riders, the lovers, and the people who keep flatland alive in Toronto and across the GTA, through practice, mentorship, jams, and by taking the sport out into the city.

What We Do

What the ATF actually does

Three things: we ride, we look for space to ride in the winter, and we take flatland to the public.

01

Ride — all season

Alexandra Park & spots across the GTA

Our home base is Alexandra Park in downtown Toronto, and we move around the GTA from there. Open sessions, no membership, no cost. If you have a bike and you’re curious, you’re already welcome. Experienced riders mentor anyone starting out, and we help with bike setup and the basics.

02

Find space to ride in the winter

Looking now — Nov to April

Toronto winters shut flatland down for five months. Riders stop, and the scene loses people every year. We’re actively looking for indoor space — a gym, a hall, a lobby, an empty room — for a few hours a week. In exchange we bring free beginner sessions, demos, and mentoring for your community.

Winter Program

Give us a floor, we’ll fill it

A gym, a hall, a lobby, an empty room. A few hours a week between November and April. That’s all it takes to keep a whole sport alive in this city through the winter.

What we bring

  • A supervised, insured program run by experienced riders
  • Hands-on beginner sessions for your members — all ages, all levels
  • Two or three performance demos during the season, free of charge
  • Mentoring and gear guidance for anyone who wants to start
  • Photos, video and social coverage credited to your venue
  • We handle setup, takedown and our own equipment

What we ask

  • A flat, dry indoor space — roughly 40 × 40 feet is plenty
  • A two to three hour weekly slot, November through April
  • Storage for a handful of bikes if one is available (not essential)
  • Whatever access arrangement fits your operations

Who we’re reaching out to

Community centres Recreation complexes School gyms Colleges & universities Boys & Girls Clubs Youth shelters Cultural centres Churches & halls Property managers Studios & warehouses

Why flatland works in a community gym

No ramps to build, no course to maintain, no noise like a skatepark. Flatland needs a bare floor and a bicycle. It is low-impact, it takes real discipline, and beginners feel progress in a single session — which is why it lands so well with youth programs.
