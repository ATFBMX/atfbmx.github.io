Winter Indoor Ride Space — Proposal

We are the Association of Toronto Flatlanders, a volunteer flatland BMX crew based in Toronto. This is a short proposal for a space-sharing arrangement over the winter months.

The problem

Flatland BMX is practiced outdoors on flat pavement. It is a low-impact, highly technical discipline — no ramps, no jumps, no track. But from roughly November to April, Toronto weather makes outdoor practice impossible.

Every winter we lose riders: beginners stop, and the people who would become the next generation of the scene disappear. A single weekly indoor session changes that completely.

What we are asking for

  • A flat, dry indoor space — approximately 40 × 40 feet is more than enough
  • A 2 to 3 hour weekly time slot, November through April
  • A gymnasium, community hall, lobby, empty classroom, studio or warehouse all work
  • Storage for a handful of bikes if available — not a requirement
  • Any reasonable access or booking arrangement you already use

What we bring in return

For your community

  • Free hands-on beginner sessions for your members, all ages
  • Two to three performance demos during the season, at no cost
  • Supervised sessions run by experienced adult riders
  • Mentoring and gear guidance for anyone interested in starting

For your venue

  • A unique program most community spaces cannot offer
  • An activity that reaches youth who do not join team sports
  • Photos, video and social media coverage credited to your venue
  • A visible, positive partnership with a local sports community

Why flatland fits an indoor community space

  • No infrastructure. No ramps to build, no course to maintain, no track to resurface.
  • Low noise, low impact. Nothing like a skatepark. Bicycle wheels on a gym floor.
  • Builds discipline. Flatland rewards patience and repetition, and beginners feel real progress in one session.
  • Reaches the kids who opt out. Not everyone wants a team sport. Flatland is individual, creative and self-paced.
  • We bring our own everything. Bikes, helmets, setup, takedown, and supervision.

Who we are

Canada has always produced flatland riders at the very top of the sport — Chase Gouin, out of Windsor, Ontario, is one of the most influential flatland riders in BMX history. The ATF is the Toronto chapter of that tradition: riders, mentors and organizers keeping flatland alive in the city and across the GTA. Our home spot is Alexandra Park, and we ride across the GTA year-round.

Insurance and supervision

Sessions are run by experienced adult riders, with a strong focus on safety and controlled progression. We are happy to work with your requirements and provide whatever documentation your organization needs, including a certificate of insurance where required.

Also available

Outside the winter program we perform paid demos and shows for events, schools, festivals and organizations, and we run hands-on initiation sessions where participants get on a bike and try flatland themselves. We also give presentations about the sport. These can be paired with a space arrangement or booked independently.

Let’s talk. If you have a room and a couple of hours a week, we can build something your community will remember.
