## Site summary

The Association of Toronto Flatlanders (ATF) is a flatland BMX crew based in Toronto, Ontario, Canada, active across the Greater Toronto Area. The site has three purposes: get indoor space for the winter, book paid demos and shows, and connect the local flatland scene.

Flatland BMX is the discipline of BMX riding done on flat ground with no ramps and no jumps. It is built on balance, bike control and variations performed on the pedals, the pegs and the back wheel.

### Who We Are ("Part of a Canadian tradition")

Canada has always produced flatland riders at the very top of the sport. Chase Gouin, out of Ontario, is one of the most influential flatland riders in the history of BMX. The ATF picks up that thread: the riders, the lovers and the people who keep flatland alive in Toronto and across the GTA, through practice, mentorship, jams, and by taking the sport out into the city.

### What We Do

Three activities:

1. **Ride, all season.** Home base is Alexandra Park (Dunbat) in downtown Toronto, plus spots across the GTA. Open sessions, no membership, no cost. Experienced riders mentor beginners and help with bike setup.
2. **Find space to ride in the winter.** Toronto winters shut flatland down for five months. The ATF is actively looking for indoor space (a gym, a hall, a lobby, an empty room) for a few hours a week, offering free beginner sessions, demos and mentoring in exchange.
3. **Shows, events and initiations.** Performance demos, hands-on initiation sessions, and presentations about the sport. For festivals, schools, community events and corporate days.

### Demos, Shows and Initiations

"We bring flatland BMX to your event. Riders perform, then hand the bikes over and let people try it themselves. No ramp, no track, no special venue: all we need is a flat, open space."

Four offers:

- **Performance demo** (20 to 45 min): A choreographed flatland show. Two to four riders rotating, high-energy and music-driven, on any flat surface. Ideal at a festival, a community event, a corporate event or a school assembly.
- **Hands-on initiation** (60 to 120 min): A demo, then everyone gets a bike. Beginner flatland basics taught in small groups: balance, rolling, first tricks. Bikes and helmets provided. Works best for youth and beginners.
- **Schools, camps and presentations** (half or full day): A full-day rotation through several groups, plus a talk about the sport, its history and where it can take you. Ties into physical education, cycling safety and goal-setting. Difficulty adapted to the age group.
- **Partnerships and sponsorships** (ongoing): Work with brands, shops and organizations who want visibility at rides and events, or who want to help build a real flatland scene in Toronto.

Pricing: not published. Every event is quoted individually based on length, travel, number of riders, and whether it is a show only or a full hands-on initiation. Quotes are sent within a few days.

### Winter Space Program ("Give us a floor, we'll fill it")

"A gym, a hall, a lobby, an empty room. A few hours a week between November and April. That's all it takes to keep a whole sport alive in this city through the winter."

**What we bring:** a supervised, insured program run by experienced riders; hands-on beginner sessions for members, all ages and levels; two or three performance demos during the season, free of charge; mentoring and gear guidance for anyone who wants to start; photos, video and social coverage credited to the venue; setup, takedown and all equipment handled by us.

**What we ask:** a flat, dry indoor space, roughly 40 x 40 feet; a two to three hour weekly slot, November through April; storage for a handful of bikes if available (not essential); whatever access arrangement fits the venue's operations.

**Who we are reaching out to:** community centres, recreation complexes, school gyms, colleges and universities, Boys and Girls Clubs, youth shelters, cultural centres, churches and halls, property managers, studios and warehouses.

**Why flatland works in a community gym:** no ramps to build, no course to maintain, no noise like a skatepark. Flatland needs a bare floor and a bicycle. It is low-impact, it takes real discipline, and beginners feel progress in a single session, which is why it lands so well with youth programs.
